# How to choose the right accessibility testing approach for test automation

![Comic-style illustration of Michaël Vanderheyden showing his latest post on the Atos Blog on a tablet](how-to-choose-the-right-accessibility-testing-approach-for-test-automation.jpg)

Building on our earlier article about [adding accessibility checks to existing test automation](/blog/beyond-compliance-building-accessibility-into-quality-with-test-automation/), I’ve co-authored a comparison with [Jörg Jakoby](https://www.linkedin.com/in/j%C3%B6rg-jakoby-93a6a8264/) of axe-core and IBM Equal Access. It looks at detection, configurability, integrations, and enforcement to help teams choose the approach that best fits their workflow.

## A quick takeaway

axe-core is a strong fit when teams want flexible rules and targeted component scans in their test workflows. IBM Equal Access may suit teams that prefer stricter defaults, built-in enforcement, and baselines for managing existing issues. Neither replaces manual accessibility testing; the right choice depends on how your team wants to find and handle issues.

This is a focused comparison, not an exhaustive list: QualWeb and Pa11y are other options, though less aligned with this in-test workflow. Evinced may also fit, but we haven’t tested or integrated it in our demo.
